## CHAPTER I
## THE HOLY QABALAH: THE FOUNDATION OF ALL

In the beginning of all study of the Hermetic Magick of the Golden Dawn standeth the Science of the Holy Qabalah, that sublime body of wisdom received by the ancient Hebrew sages and perfected by the illuminated Adepts of Christendom. Without a firm grasp of this underlying philosophy, the rituals and ceremonies are but an empty mummery; with it, they become a living language whereby the Soul may commune with the Divine.

The Qabalah teacheth that the Ineffable One, the Limitless Light which is called in Hebrew **Ain Soph Aur**, concentrated a point of its own infinite radiance, and from this primordial point emanated the Ten Sephiroth, or Numerations, together with the Paths that connect them, forming the Celestial Tree known as **Otz Chiim**, the Tree of Life. This Tree is the blueprint of all Creation, the macrocosmic pattern after which the microcosm of Man was formed, and the map of the spiritual journey that the Aspirant must undertake.

**The Ten Sephiroth** are:

1. **Kether**, the Crown, the first emanation, the primal Will.
2. **Chokmah**, Wisdom, the supernal Father.
3. **Binah**, Understanding, the supernal Mother.
4. **Chesed**, Mercy, the benevolent ruler.
5. **Geburah**, Severity, the warrior of justice.
6. **Tiphareth**, Beauty, the harmonising centre, the Son.
7. **Netzach**, Victory, the sphere of the emotions and nature-forces.
8. **Hod**, Splendour, the sphere of the intellect and magic.
9. **Yesod**, Foundation, the astral plane, the seat of the Nephesh.
10. **Malkuth**, the Kingdom, the material world, the sphere of the elements.

These Sephiroth are arranged in three Pillars: the right-hand Pillar of Mercy (Chokmah, Chesed, Netzach), male and expansive; the left-hand Pillar of Severity (Binah, Geburah, Hod), female and restrictive; and the central Pillar of Mildness or Equilibrium (Kether, Tiphareth, Yesod, Malkuth), which is the path of the Adept. The student must ever strive for the balance of the Middle Pillar, avoiding the extremes of force and form, and seeking that perfect equilibrium which is the secret of the Great Work.

**The Four Worlds**

These Ten Sephiroth are reflected in each of the Four Worlds of manifestation, which descend in density from the purest Spirit to grossest Matter:

- **Atziluth**, the Archetypal World of pure Divinity, corresponding to the element of Fire and the letter Yod of the Tetragrammaton.
- **Briah**, the Creative World of the Archangels, corresponding to Water and the first Heh.
- **Yetzirah**, the Formative World of the Angelic hosts, corresponding to Air and the Vau.
- **Assiah**, the Material World of the Elements and mankind, corresponding to Earth and the final Heh.

Every magical operation enacted in Assiah hath its correspondences in the higher worlds; the vibration of a Divine Name in Yetzirah stirreth the appropriate Angelic force, which in Briah awakeneth the Archangelic intelligence, which in Atziluth toucheth the very Godhead. Thus the Magus worketh from below to above, and the power descendeth from above to below. This is the meaning of the Hermetic axiom, “As above, so below.”

**The Flaming Sword and the Serpent of Wisdom**

The order of emanation is traced by the **Flaming Sword**, which descends from Kether through Chokmah, Binah, Chesed, Geburah, Tiphareth, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, and finally Malkuth. The path of return, the ascent of the Aspirant, is symbolised by the **Serpent of Wisdom**, which, coiled in Malkuth, riseth up the central paths and the twenty-two connecting paths of the Tree, absorbing the virtues of each Sephirah until it reacheth the Crown. These twenty-two paths correspond to the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet, the twenty-two Major Arcana of the Tarot, the twenty-two elements of astrology, and a host of other correspondences which the earnest student shall commit to memory.

**The Hebrew Alphabet and the Tarot**

The Hebrew alphabet is the sacred language of the Qabalah, each letter being a power in itself. The twenty-two Paths are assigned to the letters as follows, with their Tarot correspondences according to the system of the Order:

- **Aleph א** — The Fool (Air)
- **Beth ב** — The Magician (Mercury)
- **Gimel ג** — The High Priestess (Moon)
- **Daleth ד** — The Empress (Venus)
- **Heh ה** — The Emperor (Aries)
- **Vau ו** — The Hierophant (Taurus)
- **Zayin ז** — The Lovers (Gemini)
- **Cheth ח** — The Chariot (Cancer)
- **Teth ט** — Strength (Leo)
- **Yod י** — The Hermit (Virgo)
- **Kaph כ** — The Wheel of Fortune (Jupiter)
- **Lamed ל** — Justice (Libra)
- **Mem מ** — The Hanged Man (Water)
- **Nun נ** — Death (Scorpio)
- **Samekh ס** — Temperance (Sagittarius)
- **Ayin ע** — The Devil (Capricorn)
- **Peh פ** — The Tower (Mars)
- **Tzaddi צ** — The Star (Aquarius)
- **Qoph ק** — The Moon (Pisces)
- **Resh ר** — The Sun (Sun)
- **Shin ש** — Judgement (Fire)
- **Tau ת** — The World (Saturn, Earth)

The student should meditate upon each letter, its form, its number, its Tarot image, and its astrological attribution until the entire Tree becometh a living temple within his consciousness. Let him construct a large diagram of Otz Chiim upon a sheet of vellum, colouring each Sephirah in its appropriate scale, and inscribe thereon the Divine Names, the Angelic choirs, the planetary symbols, and the cardinal virtues. Such a diagram, contemplated daily, will serve as a wondrous aid to the memory and a portal to the higher vision.

**The Divine Names and the Powers**

Each Sephirah hath its own Name of God, Archangel, Angelic choir, and mundane chakra. These are the principal Names used in the invocations of the Pentagram and Hexagram rituals:

- **Kether**: Eheieh (אהיה), Archangel Metatron, Choir Chayoth ha-Qadesh.
- **Chokmah**: Yod-Heh-Vau-Heh (יהוה), Archangel Ratziel, Auphanim.
- **Binah**: Yod-Heh-Vau-Heh Elohim (יהוה אלהים), Archangel Tzaphkiel, Aralim.

- **Geburah**: Elohim Gibor (אלהים גבר), Archangel Kamael, Seraphim.

- **Netzach**: Yod-Heh-Vau-Heh Tzabaoth (יהוה צבאות), Archangel Haniel, Elohim.
- **Hod**: Elohim Tzabaoth (אלהים צבאות), Archangel Michael, Beni Elohim.
- **Yesod**: Shaddai El Chai (שדי אל חי), Archangel Gabriel, Kerubim.
- **Malkuth**: Adonai ha-Aretz (אדני הארץ), Archangel Sandalphon, Ashim.

Let the student learn to vibrate these Names with the full force of his will, as shall be taught in due course. The correct vibration of a Divine Name is itself a magical act of the highest order, for it draweth down the Light of the corresponding Sephirah into the sphere of the operator.

## CHAPTER II
## THE SYMBOLISM OF THE TEMPLE AND THE ROBE OF THE ADEPT

The Temple in which the ceremonies of the Golden Dawn are conducted is a representation of the Universe itself. Its floor, walls, and ceiling are arranged according to the cardinal points and the celestial spheres, and every object within it beareth a symbolic meaning which linketh the microcosm of the ceremony to the macrocosm of Creation. Even the solitary student, who perforce must establish his Temple in a private chamber, should endeavour to arrange his working space in conformity with these principles, that his environment may speak to his soul the language of the Mysteries.

**The Arrangement of the Temple**

The Altar standeth in the centre, or slightly towards the East, and upon it are placed the elemental weapons and the symbols of the grade being worked. The four quarters are marked by the elemental Tablets or symbols: in the East, an upright yellow triangle for Air; in the South, a red triangle pointing upward for Fire; in the West, a blue triangle pointing downward for Water; in the North, a black or green triangle or a circle for Earth. These may be drawn upon paper or painted upon small wooden stands.

Above the Altar, or upon the eastern wall, is placed the Banner of the East, bearing the symbol of the Rose and Cross, the Hexagram, or the Calvary Cross, according to the nature of the work. A lamp representing the Hidden Light of Kether is suspended or placed in the East.

The Magus, robed and armed, standeth in the centre of the Temple, facing the direction appropriate to the operation. The Circle, whether marked physically upon the floor or traced in the Astral Light by the wand, is the boundary between the profane world and the consecrated sphere of working. It is the sphere of Malkuth purified and uplifted.

**The Robe and Regalia**

The Robe of the Aspirant is the symbol of his spiritual condition. The Neophyte weareth a black robe, signifying the darkness of matter and the ignorance of the uninitiated, bound with a cord representing the three principles of the Order. As the student advanceth through the grades, the colour of his robe changeth to correspond with the Sephirah or element of that grade: the Zelator (Earth, Malkuth) weareth a robe of citrine, russet, olive, and black; the Theoricus (Air, Yesod) a robe of violet or purple; the Practicus (Water, Hod) a robe of orange; the Philosophus (Fire, Netzach) a robe of bright green; and the Adeptus Minor (Tiphareth) a robe of yellow or golden hue, with a mantle of white.

The student who worketh solitarily may adopt a simple white linen robe as a universal vestment of purity, and gird himself with a cord of white, red, and blue threads, representing Chesed, Geburah, and the reconciling power of Tiphareth. Upon his breast may be worn the Lamen, a symbol of his magical motto and the work he undertaketh, suspended from a collar of the appropriate colours.

The Head is bound with a fillet or circlet, or covered with a hood, for the power of the spirit is concentrated in the crown. The feet are shod with simple slippers, for the ground whereon the Magus treadeth is holy.

**The Sign and Token of the Grade**

Each grade of the Order hath its own Signs, Tokens, and Words, which are communicated in the Ceremony of Advancement. Though the detailed manner of their conferral is sealed by oath, the student may nevertheless understand their general significance. The Neophyte Signs are the Saluting Sign (the Sign of Horus, the Enterer) and the Sign of Silence (the Sign of Harpocrates). These two Signs are performed in the daily Adoration and in all banishing rituals. The Enterer signifieth the projection of the will; by it the Magus “shooteth forth” his astral force into the symbol or direction he willeth. The Sign of Silence sealeth the operation and draweth back the force, establishing equilibrium.

Let the student practise these Signs: Standing erect, step forward with the left foot, thrust out both hands at eye level as if projecting force, and envision the current of Light streaming forth; then immediately bring the left foot back, place the right forefinger upon the lip, and assume an attitude of complete stillness. These two gestures, performed with full intention, condense and refine the Astral Light and are of the very essence of operative magic.

## CHAPTER III
## THE INSTRUMENTS OF THE ART

The Magical Instruments of the Golden Dawn are not mere ornaments, but living extensions of the will of the Adept, consecrated by solemn ceremony and charged with the power of the appropriate Sephirah or Element. They are the weapons with which the operator contendeth against the unbalanced forces of the Astral Plane, and the chalices into which he receiveth the divine influx. The principal instruments are here described, together with the manner of their preparation.

**The Lotus Wand**

The Lotus Wand is the principal weapon of the Adeptus Minor. It is a slender rod of almond or hazel wood, wound with bands of colour corresponding to the Sephiroth and the zodiacal signs. At its upper extremity is a lotus flower, within which may be placed a small crystal or a gilded cone. The wand representeth the Middle Pillar of the Tree of Life, and its bands of colour enable the Adept to direct the force of any Sephirah or Path through the wand by grasping it at the appropriate band. The band for Malkuth is at the base, coloured in the four colours of Earth; Yesod is violet; Hod orange; Netzach green; Tiphareth rose or golden; Geburah red; Chesed blue; Binah black; Chokmah grey; and Kether pure white. The wand is consecrated by a ceremony of invocation and by anointing with the appropriate oils, and is thereafter kept wrapped in silk of a pure colour.

**The Magical Sword**

The Sword is the weapon of Geburah, the Sephirah of severity and justice. It is employed in all banishings and in the commanding of wayward spirits. The blade should be of steel, cross-hilted, with a black hilt. Upon the blade or the hilt are engraved the Divine Names and the Sigils of the appropriate Archangels. The Sword is the symbol of the intellect which cleaveth asunder the veils of illusion; it is also the weapon of the Kerub of the East, the Eagle, which flieth nearest the Throne.

**The Chalice**

The Cup or Chalice is the weapon of Chesed, Mercy, and the receptive power of the Magus. It is commonly made of glass, crystal, or silver, and is mounted upon a stem. It receiveth the holy water, the wine, or the elixir consecrated in the ceremonies. The Cup correspondeth to the element of Water and to the letter Heh of the Tetragrammaton. By the Cup, the Magus draweth down the influence of the supernal Mother Binah into the sphere of the operation.

**The Pentacle**

The Pantacle, or Pentacle, is a disc of wax, wood, or metal, painted or engraved with the symbols of Earth and of Malkuth. It is the weapon of the material plane, the foundation of all works. The Pantacle of the Adept beareth upon its face the Hexagram and the Pentagram, together with the Sigils of the Elemental Kings and the Names of Adonai ha-Aretz. In certain workings, smaller Pantacles of specific design are employed, painted according to the colour scales of the Tree of Life.

**The Lamp**

The Lamp, suspended from the ceiling or placed upon the Altar, is the symbol of the Hidden Light of Kether, the Ain Soph Aur. It is the undying flame of the spirit. In all works of theurgy, the Lamp burneth continuously, representing the perpetual presence of the Divine.

**The Elemental Weapons of the Outer Order**

In the grades of the Outer Order, the student constructeth and consecrateth an elemental weapon appropriate to his grade: the Zelator prepareth the Earth Pantacle; the Theoricus the Air Dagger; the Practicus the Water Cup; the Philosophus the Fire Wand. These four weapons, when placed about the Altar in their correct quarters, form the Four Magical Implements of the Adept, and their combined use in the Supreme Ritual of the Pentagram representeth the full sovereignty of the Magus over the Elements.

**Consecration**

Every instrument must be consecrated before use. The method of consecration involveth purification by water and by fire, the tracing of the appropriate Pentagram or Hexagram over it, the vibration of the relevant Divine Names, and the anointing with holy oil. The ceremony is performed within a properly cast Circle, and the instrument is charged with a clear statement of purpose. A specimen formula is as follows:

Take the instrument in thy right hand. Stand facing East. Perform the Qabalistic Cross. Sprinkle the instrument with pure water, saying: “I purify thee with water, that thou mayest be clean in the service of the Light.” Pass it thrice through the smoke of incense, saying: “I consecrate thee with fire, that thy virtue may be as a burning flame.” Trace over it the invoking Pentagram of the element to which it belongeth, vibrating the Divine Name thereof. Hold the instrument aloft and cry: “By the Name of [Divine Name], I declare thee duly consecrated to the Great Work, a fit vessel for the power of [Sephirah or Element]. Amen.”

## CHAPTER IV
## THE RITUALS OF THE PENTAGRAM

The Pentagram, or five-pointed star, is the supreme symbol of the dominion of Spirit over the four Elements. It is traced in the Air with the finger, the wand, or the sword, and by its virtue the Magus summoneth or banishseth the elemental forces. The Golden Dawn system teacheth two principal grades of Pentagram ritual: the Lesser, which is the daily guard of the Aspirant, and the Supreme, which is employed for specific elemental invocations.

**The Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram**

This ritual, called simply the L. B. R. P., is the very cornerstone of the Operative Work. It should be performed at least once each day, upon rising in the morning and before retiring at night, and additionally before any magical ceremony whatsoever. Its purpose is to cleanse the sphere of the operator, to establish a wall of astral protection about the Temple, and to equilibrate the elemental forces within the microcosm.

The ritual proceedeth thus:

1. **The Qabalistic Cross.** Standing facing East, touch thy forehead with thy right index finger or the wand, and vibrate: “A-TAH.” Touch thy breast, vibrate: “MALKUTH.” Touch thy right shoulder, vibrate: “VE-GEBURAH.” Touch thy left shoulder, vibrate: “VE-GEDULAH.” Clasp thy hands upon thy breast, fingers interlocked, and say: “LE-OLAHM, AMEN.” This establishs the Pillar of Light through the operator and aligneth him with Kether and Malkuth.

2. **The Pentagrams and Names.** Advance to the East. Trace a large Banishing Pentagram of Earth (starting from the left hip, upward to the forehead, down to the right hip, to the left shoulder, to the right shoulder, back to the left hip) in blue or white flame. In the centre of the Pentagram, vibrate the Divine Name “YOD-HEH-VAU-HEH.” Pierce the centre with thy wand or finger, and assume the Sign of the Enterer. Turn towards the South, tracing a quarter-circle of light with thy outstretched arm. Trace the same Pentagram, vibrate “ADONAI,” and give the Enterer Sign. Turn to the West, trace, vibrate “EHEIEH,” Enterer Sign. Turn to the North, trace, vibrate “AGLA,” Enterer Sign. Return to the East, completing the circle of light.

3. **Invocation of the Archangels.** Standing in the centre, facing East, extend thy arms in the form of a cross and intone:
   “Before me RAPHAEL; behind me GABRIEL; at my right hand MICHAEL; at my left hand AURIEL. For about me flames the Pentagram, and within me shineth the six-rayed Star.”
   Visualise the four Archangels as towering figures of luminous colour: Raphael in yellow and violet, bearing a Caduceus; Gabriel in blue and orange, bearing a Cup; Michael in red and green, bearing a Sword; Auriel in citrine, olive, russet, and black, bearing a Pentacle.

4. **The Qabalistic Cross is repeated.**

This simple yet profound ceremony, when practised regularly, buildeth about the Aspirant a radiant Sphere of Sensation which is impervious to hostile forces and gradually refined in nature. The student is admonished not to hurry through the ritual, but to dwell upon each vibration and each visualisation with full intent, for the power lieth not in the words but in the consciousness they awaken.

**The Lesser Invoking Ritual of the Pentagram**

When the student wisheth to invoke elemental force for a constructive purpose, he performeth the Lesser Invoking Ritual of the Pentagram. The procedure is identical to the Banishing, save that the Pentagrams are traced in the Invoking manner: for Earth, beginning from the right hip upward to the forehead, and so forth. The names are vibrated as before, and the Archangels are invoked. This form is used as a preliminary to all works of theurgy, prayer, and meditation, and may be employed daily in alternation with the Banishing form to cultivate a balanced rapport with the Elements.

**The Supreme Ritual of the Pentagram**

The Supreme Ritual of the Pentagram is a far more elaborate operation, involving the use of the four Elemental Pentagrams (Spirit, Air, Fire, Water, Earth) and the appropriate Divine Names, God-forms, and Kerubic Signs. It is the full ceremonial invocation or banishing of the elemental powers, and is performed with the Lotus Wand or the Sword, in conjunction with the Elemental Tablets. The student is directed to study the ritual in the official documents of the Order, as its complete exposition is beyond the scope of this treatise. Suffice it to say that the Supreme Ritual is the key to controlling the Elemental Kings and their realms, and must be approached with the utmost purity of intention.

## CHAPTER V
## THE RITUALS OF THE HEXAGRAM AND THE ROSE CROSS

Whereas the Pentagram governeth the elemental forces of the Earth and the four quarters, the Hexagram, or six-rayed Star, is the symbol of the Macrocosm, the planetary powers, and the Sephirah of Tiphareth. It is formed by the interlacement of the red triangle of Fire with the blue triangle of Water, signifying the union of the masculine and feminine potencies in the heart-centre.

**The Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Hexagram**

The L. B. R. H. is commonly performed immediately after the Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram, to seal the Sphere of Sensation with the planetary and zodiacal harmonies. It is particularly efficacious in preparing the Temple for meditations upon the Paths, for scrying, and for rituals of a celestial nature.

The method is as follows:
1. Perform the Qabalistic Cross.
2. Stand in the centre, facing East. Trace the Banishing Hexagram of Fire (two equilateral triangles, the upper point of the fire triangle downward from the left, etc.) in golden light, and in the centre vibrate “ARARITA” (ארריתא), a Notariqon of the phrase “Achad Rosh Achdotho Rosh Ichudo Temurahzo Achad,” meaning “One is His beginning, one is His individuality, His permutation is one.”
3. Turn to the South, trace the Banishing Hexagram of Earth, vibrate “ARARITA.”
4. West: Banishing Hexagram of Air, “ARARITA.”
5. North: Banishing Hexagram of Water, “ARARITA.”
6. Return to the East, completing the Circle.
7. Extend the arms and invoke the planetary forces by appropriate names, or simply affirm the unity of the Divine.
8. Repeat the Qabalistic Cross.

The vibration of “ARARITA” is a mighty adjuration, uniting the Seven Palaces of the Yetziratic World under the sovereignty of the One. The student will do well to meditate upon the mystery of this word.

**The Analysis of the Keyword**

Upon the conclusion of the Hexagram Ritual, it is customary in the Order to perform the Analysis of the Keyword, a ceremony which analyseth the word “INRI” into its Qabalistic components and leadeth the consciousness through the portal of the Cross. The steps are:

1. Raise the wand and say: “Let us analyse the Key Word. I. N. R. I.”
2. Trace the letters in the air: Yod (י), Nun (נ), Resh (ר), Yod (י). Say: “Yod, Nun, Resh, Yod. Virgo, Isis, Mighty Mother; Scorpio, Apophis, Destroyer; Sol, Osiris, Slain and Risen. Isis, Apophis, Osiris: I. A. O.”
3. Spread the arms: “The Sign of Osiris Slain.”
4. Raise the arms in the Sign of the Cross: “L. V. X., Lux, the Light of the Cross.”
5. Perform the Signs of the Grades: The Sign of Mourning (Osiris Slain), the Sign of the Portal, the Sign of the Adeptus Minor, and the Sign of the Enterer, returning each time to the Sign of Silence.

This analysis worketh a profound alchemy within the soul, uniting the Egyptian god-forms with the Christian mystery and the Light of Tiphareth. It is a most potent preparation for the higher operations.

**The Rose Cross Ritual**

The Ritual of the Rose Cross is a ceremony of profound peace and protection, which encloseth the operator in a sphere of rosy light, the vibration of the Rose and the Cross. It is employed when a condition of extreme harmony and secrecy is desired, and its effect is to calm all astral disturbance and to render the operator invisible to unbalanced forces. It consisteth of the tracing of the Calvary Crosses in the quarters, each accompanied by the appropriate vibration, and the circumambulation of the Temple while intoning the sacred formula. The student is advised to learn this ritual from the official papers of the Order, for it is a jewel of the Adept’s treasury.

## CHAPTER VI
## THE ANALYSIS OF THE KEYWORD (IN EXTENSO)

The ceremony of the Analysis of the Keyword, briefly alluded to in the preceding chapter, meriteth a full description, for it is itself a complete ritual of the inner planes. The Aspirant who performeth it daily in conjunction with the Pentagram and Hexagram rituals will find his astral senses quickened and his understanding of the central mystery of the Western Tradition deepened.

Begin as usual with the Qabalistic Cross. Then, taking the wand in the right hand, elevate it and pronounce with full vibration: “Let us analyse the Key Word.”

With the wand, trace the letter **Yod** (י) in the Air before thee. Visualise it in brilliant yellow light. Say: “Yod, Virgo, Isis, Mighty Mother.”

Trace **Nun** (נ) in bright blue flame: “Nun, Scorpio, Apophis, Destroyer.”

Trace **Resh** (ר) in golden radiance: “Resh, Sol, Osiris, Slain and Risen.”

Trace a second **Yod** (י) as before: “Yod.”

Now, bring the wand to the centre of the four letters and trace a great Cross of Light, saying: “Isis, Apophis, Osiris — I. A. O.”

Extend the arms wide, the feet together, and visualise the form of the mummified Osiris wrapped in white bands. Hold this Sign of Osiris Slain, saying silently the words: “He is dead.”

Raise the arms slowly to form a Cross with the body, and intone: “L. V. X., Lux, the Light of the Cross.” Visualise a brilliant stream of white light descending upon the head and filling the entire Sphere.

Now give the Signs of the Grades in succession:

- **Sign of Mourning** (Adeptus Minor): Hands clasped over the chest, head bowed. “The Sign of the Mourning of Osiris.”
- **Sign of the Portal** (the Veil of Paroketh): Standing erect, hands raised to the eyes as if parting a veil. “The Sign of the Portal of the Vault of the Adepti.”
- **Sign of the Adeptus Minor** (4° = 7°): The Sign of the Enterer given once again, but with the right hand alone, the left hand covering the heart. “The Sign of the Adeptus Minor.”
- **Sign of Silence**, sealing the work.

Conclude with the Qabalistic Cross.

By this ceremony the Aspirant identifieth himself with the slain and resurrected God, traverseth the Veil of Paroketh, and receiveth the influx of the Light of Tiphareth. It is a rite of high theurgy, and its regular performance will establish the Ruach (the rational mind) in the service of the Neschamah (the spiritual intuition).

## CHAPTER VII
## THE GRADES OF THE OUTER ORDER

The Golden Dawn in its Outer manifestation compriseth a system of ten grades corresponding to the Sephiroth of the Tree of Life, the first five of which constitute the Elemental Grades through which every Aspirant must pass. Each grade conferreth upon the student a body of occult knowledge, a set of symbols, and a specific magical task; and by the diligent study and practice proper to each grade, the Aspirant gradually purifieth and equilibrateth the elements of his own nature.

**The Neophyte (0° = 0°)**

The Hall of the Neophyte is situated symbolically at the intersection of the paths of Malkuth, and its purpose is to admit the Candidate to the Light. The knowledge imparted at this stage includeth the Hebrew alphabet, the L. B. R. P., the Qabalistic Cross, the Signs of the Enterer and Silence, and the Mystic Repast. The Neophyte learneth to banish and to purify, and to set his foot upon the Path.

**The Zelator (1° = 10°)**

The grade of Zelator correspondeth to Malkuth, the sphere of the elements. The student is instructed in the earthly aspect of the Tree, the Four Worlds in Assiah, the geomantic figures, and the administration of the Temple. His practical work consisteth in the construction and consecration of the Earth Pantacle, and in the daily performance of the L. R. P. with special attention to the element of Earth. The Zelator meditateth upon the Sephirah Malkuth, its Divine Name Adonai ha-Aretz, and the Archangel Sandalphon, and learneth to ground his spiritual aspirations in practical charity and bodily discipline.

**The Theoricus (2° = 9°)**

Attributed to Yesod, the sphere of the Moon and the Astral Plane, the Theoricus grade openeth the inner vision. The student constructeth the Air Dagger, learneth the astrology of the natal chart, and beginneth the practice of rising on the planes. He meditateth upon Shaddai El Chai and the Archangel Gabriel, and his daily regimen includeth the Middle Pillar exercise, the circulation of the Light, and the first experiments in skrying.

**The Practicus (3° = 8°)**

The grade of Practicus is attributed to Hod, the sphere of the intellect and of Mercury. The student prepareth the Water Cup, studieth the Tarot in its divinatory and Qabalistic aspects, and deepeneth his knowledge of the Hebrew letters. He now learneth the Lesser Ritual of the Hexagram and the formulae of invocation. His meditation is upon Elohim Tzabaoth and the Archangel Michael, and his daily work is to train the rational mind to become a translucent vehicle of the higher self.

**The Philosophus (4° = 7°)**

The Philosophus grade correspondeth to Netzach, the sphere of Venus and the fiery emotions. The Fire Wand is constructed and consecrated. The student perfecteth his knowledge of the Qabalistic correspondences and the geomantic arts, and his practical work involveth the skrying of the Tattwa symbols and the elemental realms. He meditateth upon YHVH Tzabaoth and the Archangel Haniel. At this stage the Aspirant must face and transmute the lower emotions, particularly those of attachment and desire, that the fire of Netzach may burn with a clear and steady flame.

**The Portal**

Between the Philosophus grade and the Adeptus Minor standeth the Portal, which is not a grade but a passage through the Veil of Paroketh. Herein the student integrateth the four elemental weapons and undergoeth a period of intense self-examination and purification. The rituals of the Portal include the analysis of the Keyword and the meditation upon the Rose and Cross. The candidate is bound to a solemn aspiration, and if he passeth the tests, he is admitted to the Second Order.

**The Adeptus Minor (5° = 6°)**

The grade of Adeptus Minor, attributed to Tiphareth, is the goal of the Outer Order’s work and the beginning of the true Great Work. In this grade the Aspirant is instructed in the practical alchemy of the soul, the consecration of the Lotus Wand, the Supreme Rituals of the Pentagram and Hexagram, and the formula of the Rose Cross. The Adeptus Minor seeketh the Knowledge and Conversation of the Holy Guardian Angel, and his daily practice is summarised in the Adoration of the Lord of the Universe, the daily vibration of the Middle Pillar, and the constant aspiration to the Light. The ceremony of the Adeptus Minor, which taketh place within the symbolic Vault of the Adepti, is the most exalted of the Order’s public rites, and its receipt marketh a true rebirth of the soul into the sphere of Beauty and Harmony.

## CHAPTER VIII
## THE GREAT WORK: PURIFICATION, ILLUMINATION, AND UNION

The ultimate objective of all Hermetic Magic is the accomplishment of the **Magnum Opus**, the Great Work, which is the transformation of the lead of the lower personality into the gold of the spiritualised consciousness. This Work proceedeth in three distinct yet overlapping stages: Purification, Illumination, and Union.

**Purification**

The First Stage consisteth in the systematic purification of the Nephesh (the animal soul) and the Ruach (the rational ego). The Aspirant, through the discipline of the Grades, identifyeth the unbalanced elements within himself, be they the inertia of Earth, the instability of Air, the volatility of Water, or the tyranny of Fire. By the daily practice of the Banishing Rituals, by fasting, prayer, and self-examination, by the confession of faults and the cultivation of the opposing virtues, the vessel of the personality is cleansed. The elemental weapons, constructed and consecrated, serve as external mirrors of this inner work: the Earth Pantacle remindeth the Aspirant of his bodily duties, the Air Dagger of the necessity of clear thought, the Water Cup of the need for emotional purity, and the Fire Wand of the discipline of the will.

**Illumination**

When the vessel is sufficiently cleansed, the Light of Tiphareth beginneth to descend and to fill the Sphere of Sensation. This is the Stage of Illumination. The Aspirant’s meditations deepen, his dreams becometh prophetic, and his waking consciousness is visited by flashes of intuitive understanding. The practice of the Middle Pillar, performed daily, quickeneth the central channel and alloweth the Light to circulate freely. The Skrying of the Tattwas and the Pathworkings of the Tree of Life open the inner vision, and the Aspirant beginneth to perceive the Angelic and Archangelic presences which surround him. At this stage, the rational mind must learn to bow before the higher wisdom of the Neschamah; the Aspirant is no longer the doer, but the instrument of the Divine Will.

**Union**

The final Stage is Union, or Knowledge and Conversation with the Holy Guardian Angel. This is the attainment of the Grade of Adeptus Minor in its fullness, when the Angel speaketh clearly to the purified Ruach and guideth the Aspirant in all things. The Angel is not an external entity, but the Higher Self, the divine spark in Tiphareth which linketh the personality to the Supernal Triad. The attainment of this Union is celebrated by the ritual of the Vault, and its permanent establishment is the work of the Adeptus Exemptus and the higher grades of the Second Order. From this centre in Tiphareth, the Adept may then, with the Angel’s guidance, approach the abyss which separateth the created world from the supernal light of the Triad.

Let the Student be not dismayed by the length of this journey. Every step faithfully taken, every daily rite performed, every moment of sincere aspiration bringeth the Goal nearer. The Great Work is not accomplished by violent effort, but by steady perseverance in the paths of Equilibrium.

## CHAPTER IX
## THE DAILY REGIMEN OF THE ASPIRANT

The daily practice of the Aspirant is the backbone of his progress. Without a regular and systematic regimen, the occasional ceremony is of little avail, and the subtle body remaineth undeveloped. The following is a recommended daily order for the student who hath attained the grade of Theoricus or beyond, to be adapted according to circumstance and the counsel of the Angel.

**Upon Rising**

1. **The Adoration to the Lord of the Universe.** Facing the rising sun (or the East), give the Sign of the Enterer, and recite:
   “Holy art Thou, Lord of the Universe! Holy art Thou, whom Nature hath not formed! Holy art Thou, the Vast and the Mighty One! Lord of the Light and of the Darkness!”
   Follow with the Sign of Silence.

2. **The Qabalistic Cross.**

3. **The Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram.**

4. **The Middle Pillar Exercise.** This is a most essential practice for the development of the central channel and the circulation of the Divine Light. Standing, visualise a brilliant sphere of white radiance above thy head (Kether). Vibrate “EHEIEH,” and draw a shaft of light from Kether down to the throat centre (Daath), vibrating “YOD-HEH-VAU-HEH ELOHIM.” From the throat, draw the light down to the heart centre (Tiphareth), vibrating “YOD-HEH-VAU-HEH ELOAH VA-DAATH.” From the heart, draw the light down to the solar plexus (Yesod), vibrating “SHADDAI EL CHAI.” Finally, draw the light down to the feet (Malkuth), vibrating “ADONAI HA-ARETZ.” These spheres of light should be visualised as radiant suns, approximately the size of a hand’s span, shining with the appropriate colours of the Sephiroth (white, grey, black, blue, red, golden, violet, etc., according to scale). Then, by the power of the will, circulate the light from the feet up the sides of the body to the crown, down the front, forming an ovoid of vibrating brilliance around the whole Sphere of Sensation.

5. **The Analysis of the Keyword.**

**During the Day**

- Let the Aspirant regularly recollect himself, and by a brief glance inward, reaffirm the presence of the Light in the heart centre. The name of the Angel may be silently invoked.
- Let every action, however mundane, be offered as an oblation to the Divine. The elemental weapons may be briefly visualised and their virtues called to mind: patience from Earth, clarity from Air, compassion from Water, courage from Fire.
- At noon, if possible, a few minutes of silent meditation upon the Sephirah of the day or the sun’s passage through the heavens.

**Upon Retiring**

1. **The Qabalistic Cross.**
2. **The Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram.** (The Banishing form is used at night to cleanse the Sphere of all astral residues accumulated during the day.)
3. **The Middle Pillar Exercise** (a gentler form, with less intense visualisation, to balance the forces before sleep).
4. **The Adoration to the Lord of the Universe.**
5. **Record of the Day:** Write a brief account in thy Magical Diary of the day’s practices, dreams, and any notable occurrences. This diary is a mirror of the soul and a precious record for the Adept’s later review.

**Weekly and Monthly Observances**

- One day each week should be set aside for a more extended magical working, such as the Supreme Ritual of the Pentagram, a skrying session, a Tarot divination, or the consecration of a talisman.
- The phases of the Moon are of great importance: the waxing moon is for invoking and building, the full moon for consecration, and the waning moon for banishing and purification.
- The Equinoxes and Solstices are high festivals of the Order, and the Aspirant should mark them with special ceremonies and a renewal of his Obligation.

## CHAPTER X
## THE PRACTICE OF SPIRITUAL VISION

Spiritual Vision, or skrying in the Spirit Vision, is the art of projecting the consciousness through the astral plane into the realm of the Sephiroth, the Elemental Tablets, or the Paths of the Tree. It is the natural extension of the concentration developed in the daily regimen, and is the chief means by which the Aspirant acquireth direct knowledge of the invisible worlds.

**Preparation**

The chamber should be darkened, save for the Lamp burning in the East. The Aspirant, robed and purified, performeth the L. B. R. P. and the L. B. R. H., and seateth himself comfortably in the centre of the Temple, facing the Altar. The Symbol to be skried — a Tattwa card, a Tarot Key, a geomantic figure, or a coloured diagram of a Sephirah — is placed before him, illuminated by a single candle or held in the mind’s eye.

**Method**

Let the Aspirant fix his gaze upon the symbol, allowing the eyes to remain relaxed and the mind still. After a few minutes, let him close his eyes and retain the after-image. To this after-image, he applyeth the formula of the “Body of Light,” also called the “Simulacrum” or “Astral Form.”

To project the Body of Light, the Aspirant vividly imagineth a duplicate of his physical form, composed of luminous cloud, standing before him. He then transfereth his consciousness into this astral double by an act of will, and seeth the symbol no longer as an external object but as a gigantic portal before him. He may now command the symbol to grow until it becometh a doorway of the appropriate size. He may trace upon it the invoking Pentagram of the element or planet, and vibrate the Divine Names. The portal will open, and he will pass through.

The Aspirant should first find himself in a landscape corresponding to the symbol: a forest for Earth, a mountain peak for Air, a lake or sea for Water, a volcanic plain for Fire. He should then, by the power of will, call for a guide — the appropriate Angel or intelligence of the realm — using the names and sigils learned in the grades. The guide will conduct him through the realm and answer his questions. All that is seen and heard must be recorded immediately upon returning to the physical body, for the memory of the astral journey fadeth swiftly.

**Tattwa Skrying**

The Hindu Tattwas, adopted by the Order, are most excellent symbols for the beginner. The five principal Tattwas are:

- **Akasha** — a black egg, symbol of Spirit.
- **Tejas** — a red triangle, Fire.
- **Vayu** — a blue circle, Air.
- **Apas** — a silver crescent, Water.
- **Prithivi** — a yellow square, Earth.

Each Tattwa openeth into a corresponding sub-plane of the Astral Light. By combining two Tattwas — for example, Fire of Water — the Aspirant may explore the subtle interpenetration of the elements. The Tattwas are placed upon the Altar and treated in the manner described, and their exploration is one of the most fruitful and harmless exercises of the inner vision.

**Pathworkings**

When proficiency in elemental skrying is attained, the Aspirant may undertake the Pathworkings, which are the skrying of the twenty-two Paths of the Tree of Life. Each Path is approached through its Tarot Key, and the journey is undertaken under the presidency of the Angel of the Path and the Hebrew letter. The Pathworkings are profound initiations, and each one conferreth a specific alchemical virtue upon the soul. The maps of the Paths, with their colour scales, intelligences, and spirits, are given in the Order’s document “The Book of the Paths,” and should be thoroughly memorised before the working.

## CHAPTER XI
## THE KNOWLEDGE AND CONVERSATION OF THE HOLY GUARDIAN ANGEL

The central quest of the Adeptus Minor, and indeed the very pivot upon which the whole spiritual life turneth, is the attainment of the Knowledge and Conversation of the Holy Guardian Angel. This Angel is the “Silent Watcher” of the soul, the Augoeides, the Higher Genius which dwelleth in Tiphareth and which constitute the true identity of the Aspirant. To attain unto the Angel is to fulfil the precept of the Oracle of Delphi: “Know Thyself.”

The methods for this attainment are various, and each Aspirant will be guided by his Angel to the particular regimen which suiteth his temperament. Nevertheless, certain general principles may be set forth.

**The Purification of the Sphere**

The Angel cannot descend into a vessel that is polluted. The Aspirant must therefore undergo a period of prolonged purification, often lasting many months, in which the diet is regulated (flesh meats and intoxicants being diminished or eliminated), the speech guarded, the thoughts watched, and the emotions calmed. The daily banishings and the Middle Pillar are performed with redoubled fervour. The Aspirant should also perform a daily “Liber Jugorum,” a practice of mortifying the will by the infliction of a mild penance upon any failure in the appointed task, such as the drawing of a cord upon the arm.

**The Invocation of the Angel**

The Aspirant may compose a personal invocation, or employ the sublime “Bornless Ritual” (the Preliminary Invocation of the Goetia), adapted to theurgic ends. The bornless ritual, beginning “Thee I invoke, the Bornless One,” calleth upon the highest divine powers and aligneth the magus with the archetype of the perfected man. It should be vibrated with immense power and feeling, preferably at dawn, and accompanied by the tracing of the Hexagram of Tiphareth and the offering of incense and light.

**The Retreat**

Many Adepts have found it necessary to withdraw from the world for a period of intensive spiritual work, following the prescription of the “Book of the Sacred Magic of Abramelin the Mage.” This operation requireth a secluded chamber, a window opening to the East, and a period of six or eighteen months of uninterrupted prayer and invocation. The Aspirant, however, who cannot withdraw so long, may perform a shortened “Abramelin” operation, dedicating a week or a month to seclusion and the daily recitation of the Bornless Ritual and the Holy Psalms.

**The Sign of the Angel**

The advent of the Angel is not marked by any common sign; it may come as a vision of light, a voice speaking in the silence, a sudden infusion of knowledge, or a pervading sense of peace and certitude. When the Angel hath been attained, the Aspirant knoweth, with a knowledge beyond all doubt, that he is not alone: the Angel walketh with him, speaketh through his intuition, and directeth his magical work unerringly.

From that moment, the Adept is no longer an Aspirant but a true Servant of the Light. His task now is to obey the Angel’s instructions, which will inevitably lead him to the purification of the lower Sephiroth, the balancing of the Pillars, and the eventual crossing of the Abyss.

## CHAPTER XII
## THE ASCENT THROUGH THE SUPERNAL TRIAD

The path beyond the grade of Adeptus Minor leadeth through the Veil of the Abyss, that great gulf which separateth the three Supernal Sephiroth from the lower seven. The Supernal Triad — Kether, Chokmah, and Binah — representeth the divine archetypes of Being, Wisdom, and Understanding, the realm of the Neschamah and the Chiah, the true spiritual soul and the life-force. The crossing of the Abyss is the supreme ordeal of the Magus, and it cannot be accomplished by any formula of the lower magic; it is a mystery of the Third Order, of which little may be written.

Nevertheless, the Aspirant who hath attained the Angel may begin to direct his aspiration towards Binah, the Understanding, which is the throne of the supernal Mother and the gateway to the Abyss. The work of this stage consisteth in the complete surrender of the Ruach (the rational self) to the Neschamah (the spiritual intuition). All that the Aspirant hath thought to be himself — his opinions, his preferences, his identity — must be surrendered into the Cup of Binah. This is the “death of the ego,” the dissolution of the false self in the sea of the Infinite.

The daily practice of the Adeptus Exemptus (7° = 4°) is a ceaseless meditation upon the Ain Soph, the Limitless Light, and the progressive dissolution of all boundaries. The rituals of the Hexagram and the Pentagram are now performed as acts of pure being, without attachment to result. The Adept cometh to understand that he is not the doer, but the instrument of Kether, the Crown.

The final attainment, the Grade of Magus (9° = 2°), is the perfect expression of the divine Word, the Chokmah which is the Wisdom of God. The Magus uttereth the Word which is his own inmost nature, and by that Word he createth a new current of spiritual influence in the world. This is a mystery which none can teach, for it is the gift of the Most High alone.

To the Aspirant who readeth these words, let it be said: Be not over-anxious concerning the distant summits. Perform the Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram daily, and all things shall be added unto you. Purify the vessel, and the Light shall descend. Seek the Angel, and the Angel will guide you. The Way is steep, but it is sure; the Path is narrow, but it leadeth unto Life.

*May the Divine Light of the Ain Soph Aur illuminate thy path, and may the Rose of Sharon bloom upon the Cross of thy heart. Amen.*

## GLOSSARY OF HERMETIC AND QABALISTIC TERMS

**Abyss, The.** The great metaphysical gulf separating the three Supernal Sephiroth from the lower seven. In the soul, it is the division between the spiritual self and the personal ego; the crossing thereof is the supreme ordeal of the Adept.

**Adept.** One who hath attained the Knowledge and Conversation of the Holy Guardian Angel and is an initiated member of the Second Order (the Rosae Rubeae et Aureae Crucis). The term implieth mastery of the elemental forces and the consecration of the personality as a vehicle of the Divine.

**Adeptus Minor (5° = 6°).** The grade attributing to Tiphareth, the first of the Second Order. The Adeptus Minor is charged with the accomplishment of the Great Work and the maintenance of the balance between the spiritual and the material.

**Adonai (אדני).** “Lord,” the Divine Name of Malkuth, often used as a substitute for the Tetragrammaton in prayer. In the L. R. P., it is vibrated in the South.

**Agla (אגלא).** A Notariqon of the Hebrew phrase “Ateh Gibor Le-Olam Adonai,” “Thou art mighty forever, O Lord.” Vibrated in the North in the Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram.

**Ain Soph Aur (אין סוף אור).** The Limitless Light, the third Veil of Negative Existence beyond Kether, from which the first concentration of manifestation proceedeth.

**Air Dagger.** The elemental weapon of the Theoricus, attributing to the element of Air and the Sephirah Yesod. It is used in rituals of the mind and in the consecration of the temple.

**Akasha.** The fifth element, Spirit, represented in the Tattwa system by a black egg. It is the substratum of the other four elements and correspondeth to the Aethyr.

**Altar.** The central piece of furniture in the Temple, representing Malkuth elevated and the union of the four elements. It is often of black wood, painted with the elemental symbols and the Calvary Cross.

**Angel, Holy Guardian.** The Higher Self, the Augoeides, the divine individuality which dwelleth in Tiphareth. Attainment of its Knowledge and Conversation is the goal of the Adeptus Minor.

**Archangels.** The four mighty beings Raphael (Air, East), Michael (Fire, South), Gabriel (Water, West), and Auriel (Earth, North) who rule the quarters in the Qabalistic system. Invoked in the L. R. P. for protection and equilibrating.

**ARARITA (ארריתא).** A Notariqon of a Hebrew phrase signifying “One is His beginning, one is His individuality, His permutation is one.” Vibrated in the Hexagram rituals.

**Assiah.** The Material World, the fourth of the Four Worlds, corresponding to the final Heh of the Tetragrammaton and to the Sephirah Malkuth.

**Astral Light.** The subtle medium which interpenetrateth the material plane and is the substance of the Body of Light, dreams, and magical phenomena. Governed by Yesod and the Moon.

**Atziluth.** The Archetypal World, the highest of the Four Worlds, corresponding to the Yod of the Tetragrammaton and to the Sephirah Kether.

**Banishing.** A ritual formula for the dispersal of unwanted forces and the purification of the sphere. The Banishing Pentagram and Hexagram are traced counter-clockwise or in the specific mode of each element.

**Binah (בינה).** The third Sephirah, Understanding. The supernal Mother, the sphere of Saturn, the Divine Name Yod-Heh-Vau-Heh Elohim.

**Body of Light.** The astral body or simulacrum projected by the Magus in skrying and travel on the planes. It is built and refined by the Middle Pillar exercise and the circulation of the Light.

**Bornless Ritual.** The “Preliminary Invocation” of the Goetia, adapted by the Order as a theurgic invocation of the highest divine power, used in the operation of the Knowledge and Conversation.

**Briah.** The Creative World, the second of the Four Worlds, corresponding to the first Heh and to the Sephirah Chokmah.

**Chesed (חסד).** The fourth Sephirah, Mercy. The sphere of Jupiter, the benevolent ruler, Divine Name El.

**Chiah (חיה).** The life-force, the second of the four parts of the soul, residing in Chokmah. It is the creative will.

**Chokmah (חכמה).** The second Sephirah, Wisdom. The supernal Father, the sphere of the zodiac, Divine Name Yod-Heh-Vau-Heh.

**Crown, the. (Kether).** The first Sephirah, the primal emanation from the Ain Soph Aur.

**Cup.** The elemental weapon of Water and the Practicus, corresponding to Chesed. It receiveth the divine influence.

**Daath (דעת).** Knowledge, the invisible Sephirah situated in the Abyss below Kether, often representing the union of Chokmah and Binah. It is the seat of the false ego when it approacheth the Supernal Triad unprepared.

**Eheieh (אהיה).** “I Am,” the Divine Name of Kether, vibrated in the West in the L. R. P.

**Elemental Tablets.** The four Watchtowers, large squares of letters and symbols representing the four elements in Yetzirah, used in Enochian magic and the Supreme Ritual of the Pentagram.

**Elements.** Fire, Water, Air, and Earth, the four archetypal modes of manifestation, ruled by the Tetragrammaton and the Kerubic angels.

**Equilibrium.** The state of perfect balance between the Pillars of Mercy and Severity, attained on the Middle Pillar. The secret of all magical progress.

**Flaming Sword.** The order of emanation of the Sephiroth, descending from Kether to Malkuth in a zigzag path.

**Geburah (גבורה).** The fifth Sephirah, Severity. The sphere of Mars, Divine Name Elohim Gibor.

**Hexagram.** The six-rayed star, symbol of the Macrocosm and Tiphareth, formed of the red triangle of Fire and the blue triangle of Water.

**Hod (הוד).** The eighth Sephirah, Splendour. The sphere of Mercury, Divine Name Elohim Tzabaoth.

**I. A. O.** Isis, Apophis, Osiris; the formula of birth, death, and resurrection, used in the Analysis of the Keyword.

**INRI.** The inscription upon the Cross of Christ, analysed Qabalistically into Yod, Nun, Resh, Yod, corresponding to Virgo, Scorpio, Sol, and the I. A. O. formula.

**Invoking.** The ritual formula for calling down a force into the sphere of the operator. The Invoking Pentagrams and Hexagrams are traced clockwise or in the specified manner.

**Kether (כתר).** See Crown.

**Kerubim.** The angelic choir of Yesod, and the four holy living creatures who support the throne of God: the Eagle (Air), the Lion (Fire), the Man (Water), and the Bull (Earth).

**Lamen.** A magical breastplate worn suspended from a collar, bearing the sigil and motto of the Adept.

**Lamp.** The eternal flame of the Spirit, suspended in the East or placed upon the Altar.

**Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram (L. R. P.).** The fundamental daily rite of purification, invulcation, and equilibrium.

**Lesser Ritual of the Hexagram (L. R. H.).** The planetary banishing or invoking ritual, employing the Hexagram and the word ARARITA.

**Lotus Wand.** The primary magical weapon of the Adeptus Minor, a wand wound with bands of colour corresponding to the Sephiroth and the zodiacal signs, representing the Middle Pillar.

**Malkuth (מלכות).** The tenth Sephirah, the Kingdom, the material world. Divine Name Adonai ha-Aretz.

**Middle Pillar, The Exercise of.** A daily practice of vibrating the Divine Names upon the central Sephiroth and circulating the Light, building the Body of Light.

**Nephesh (נפש).** The animal soul, the lowest part of the soul, seated in Yesod.

**Neschamah (נשמה).** The spiritual soul, the third part, seated in Binah, the faculty of divine intuition.

**Netzach (נצח).** The seventh Sephirah, Victory. The sphere of Venus, Divine Name YHVH Tzabaoth.

**Neophyte (0° = 0°).** The first grade of the Golden Dawn, the entry into the Path of Light.

**Notariqon.** A Qabalistic method of forming acronyms from the letters of a phrase.

**Otz Chiim (עץ חיים).** The Tree of Life, the diagram of the ten Sephiroth and twenty-two Paths.

**Pantacle (Pentacle).** The elemental weapon of Earth, a disc inscribed with symbols and names, used to ground and contain force.

**Paroketh, Veil of.** The Veil of the Temple separating the lower grades of the Outer Order from the Adeptus Minor and Tiphareth.

**Pathworkings.** The practice of skrying the twenty-two Paths of the Tree of Life through their Tarot Keys.

**Pentagram.** The five-pointed star, symbol of the Spirit ruling the four Elements. The Banishing Pentagram of Earth is the principal shield of the Aspirant.

**Philosophus (4° = 7°).** The grade of Netzach, the Fire of the Outer Order.

**Portal, The.** The passage through the Veil of Paroketh, involving the integration of the elemental weapons and the Analysis of the Keyword.

**Practicus (3° = 8°).** The grade of Hod, the Water of the Outer Order.

**Qabalistic Cross.** The fundamental alignment of the operator with Kether and Malkuth, performed by touching the forehead, breast, right shoulder, left shoulder, and clasping the hands.

**Qabalah.** The esoteric tradition of the Hebrews, the philosophical and symbolic basis of the Golden Dawn system.

**Rose Cross Ritual.** A ceremony of peace and concealment, surrounding the operator with a sphere of rosy light.

**Ruach (רוח).** The rational mind, the second part of the soul, seated in the five Sephiroth around Tiphareth.

**Sephiroth (ספירות).** The ten emanations of the Divine Light, forming the Tree of Life.

**Serpent of Wisdom.** The path of return, ascending the Tree from Malkuth to Kether, absorbing the virtues of each Sephirah.

**Sign of the Enterer.** The Saluting Sign of the Neophyte, a gesture of projecting the will forward with the left foot advanced and both hands thrust out.

**Sign of Silence.** The Sign of Harpocrates, the right forefinger to the lip, sealing the work and restoring equilibrium.

**Skrying.** The practice of spiritual vision, projecting the consciousness through the Astral Light into the symbolic realms.

**Supernal Triad.** The three highest Sephiroth: Kether, Chokmah, Binah, representing the divine archetypes beyond the Abyss.

**Tattwa.** Hindu elemental symbols adopted by the Order for skrying: Akasha, Tejas, Vayu, Apas, Prithivi.

**Temple.** The consecrated space wherein the magical ceremonies are enacted.

**Tetragrammaton (יהוה).** The four-lettered Name of God, Yod-Heh-Vau-Heh, representing the four elements and the four worlds.

**Theoricus (2° = 9°).** The grade of Yesod, the Air of the Outer Order.

**Tiphareth (תפארת).** The sixth Sephirah, Beauty, the sphere of the Sun and the seat of the Holy Guardian Angel. Divine Name YHVH Eloah va-Daath.

**Tree of Life.** See Otz Chiim.

**Vault of the Adepti.** The symbolic chamber of the Adeptus Minor, representing the tomb of Osiris and the alchemical furnace of transmutation.

**Wand, Fire.** The elemental weapon of the Philosophus, representing the will and the element of Fire.
